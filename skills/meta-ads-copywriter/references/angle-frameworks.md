# Ad angle frameworks

Use these as starting points for generating genuinely distinct variants, not a checklist to mechanically fill in.

- **Pain-point-led** — open on the problem the audience already recognizes in themselves ("Still manually copying data between spreadsheets every week?") before introducing the solution.
- **Social-proof-led** — open on evidence of others' success or adoption ("Join 12,000 teams who switched off spreadsheets this year") — only use real numbers the user has provided, never invented ones.
- **Curiosity-led** — open with a specific, concrete detail that creates an information gap the reader wants closed ("The one spreadsheet formula costing your team 4 hours a week") — avoid vague clickbait ("You won't believe this trick") which performs worse and reads as low-trust.
- **Direct-offer-led** — skip the narrative entirely and lead with the concrete value prop and offer, best for retargeting audiences who already know the brand and just need the nudge.
- **Contrarian/myth-bust** — open by challenging a common assumption the audience holds ("Your CRM isn't the problem. Your data entry process is.") — works well for category-education products.

Pick 3 that fit the specific product and audience rather than always defaulting to the same three — e.g. cold/top-of-funnel audiences respond better to pain-point or curiosity angles, while retargeting audiences respond better to direct-offer or social-proof.
